01 / THE QUESTION

Seeing more.
Knowing more?

Hotel imagery can do at least two jobs: reveal a room’s useful details and create a sense of place. Those jobs can overlap. This study asks whether they can be separated—and whether they lead to different decisions.

PRIMARY OUTCOME

Understanding

How many room features can someone identify correctly? Six questions, scored against verified room facts.

SECONDARY OUTCOME

Confidence

How sure are they about their answers? A more assured decision may still be an inaccurate one.

A stated intention to book is a secondary measure. It cannot establish a purchase or a return on photography spending.

04 / DESIGN BEFORE OUTCOMES

A protocol you can inspect.

How the comparison would work

One verified room. Four photo sets, six slots each. Identical price, policies, copy and layout. Assign adults with recent hotel-booking experience in concealed balanced blocks. Measure six room facts before asking about confidence, appeal or the intended manipulation.

Clarity changes coverage; atmosphere changes aesthetic presentation. An independent pretest must show the intended difference and rule out a practically meaningful cross-effect. The diagrams here do not meet that requirement.

Why 352 complete outcomes?

A normal-approximation power plan assumes d = 0.30, 80% power and two-sided α = .05 for the clarity main effect. That would mean 0.45 more correct items if the score SD were 1.5—an unverified assumption. At d = .20 the target is 788; at d = .40 it is 200.

A fixed ceiling of 416 assignments allows for a planning assumption of 15% missing outcomes. We would not keep recruiting based on significance. This does not power a small interaction or a revenue claim.

What happens to missing responses?

An unanswered item is missing. “Cannot tell” is an explicit response and scores zero against the verified room fact. The analysis keeps the assigned roster, reports attrition by condition and gives conservative bounds for missing scores between 0 and 6. It never turns a dropout into an observed zero.

The primary complete-outcome contrast gives equal weight to each atmosphere level. It is accompanied by cell-level uncertainty and full-roster missing-data bounds. Technical failures remain visible.

What would count as a finding?

A randomized comparison could support a causal claim about the assigned photo sets and room-information accuracy in that sample. Generalization to other rooms, audiences and actual booking behavior would require more work. Confidence and stated booking intention remain separate from purchases.

All current interactions are demo events. There is no participant evidence to interpret and no basis for estimating photography’s revenue return.
